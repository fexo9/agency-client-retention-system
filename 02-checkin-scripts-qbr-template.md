# 30/60/90-DAY CHECK-IN CALL SCRIPTS
## Plus the Quarterly Business Review (QBR) Meeting Template

**How to use this document:** The first 90 days are where churn happens — the Moxo 2026 B2B Retention Report found that 43% of client churn occurs in the first 90 days. These three check-in calls are your early-warning radar. Run them on schedule for every new client, no exceptions. The QBR template is for quarter-end with your top accounts (or every account over your minimum retainer threshold).

**Scheduling rule:** Book the 30-day call on the day the contract is signed. Book the 60- and 90-day calls at the end of each preceding call. Calendar holds beat good intentions.

**Placeholders:** Replace everything in {brackets} before the call. Never improvise the questions — they're written to surface risk.

---

## THE 30-DAY CHECK-IN — "The Foundation Call"

**Timing:** 30 days after kickoff. 25 minutes. On video, camera on.

**Goal:** Confirm the client feels heard and that early deliverables landed well. This is the call where you catch onboarding damage while it's still cheap to fix.

**Agenda:**
1. Welcome + context recap (3 min)
2. "How does it feel so far?" — the open question (5 min)
3. Walk through each open deliverable's status (10 min)
4. Surface and name any friction (5 min)
5. Confirm next 30 days + book the 60-day call (2 min)

**Script — opening:**

> "Hi {ClientName}, thanks for the time. I wanted to personally check in now that we're a month in. I'll keep this to 25 minutes. First — from your side, how does the work feel so far?"

**Risk-surfacing questions (ask verbatim, then shut up and listen):**
1. "If you had to describe our first month to your {boss/partner}, what would you say?" — *Reveals the story they're telling internally about you. If the answer is thin or vague, confidence is low.*
2. "Has anything so far taken longer or felt harder than you expected?" — *Surfaces friction they haven't formalized into a complaint yet.*
3. "Is there anyone else on your team who should be in these updates but isn't?" — *Uncovers invisible stakeholders — the people who kill renewals from the sidelines.*
4. "On a scale of 1 to 10, how confident are you right now that we're going to hit the {QuarterlyGoal}?" — *Anything below 7 is an early-warning flag. Ask: "What would move it to a 9?"*

**What to listen for:**
- "It's fine" / "No complaints" with no specifics → disengagement. Probe: "What would make it excellent instead of fine?"
- Mentions of a previous agency → they're benchmarking you against a past disappointment (or a past relationship they miss). Find out which.
- Blame directed at *their* team ("our designer is slow") → scope friction is coming; document it.

**After the call:** Log the confidence score (question 4) in your Client Health Score Tracker. Anything below 7 drops the engagement indicator.

## THE 60-DAY CHECK-IN — "The Momentum Call"

**Timing:** 60 days after kickoff. 25 minutes. On video.

**Goal:** Shift the conversation from *activity* (what we've done) to *impact* (what it's doing for their business). This is where you start building the renewal case.

**Agenda:**
1. Quick recap of impact so far — lead with THEIR numbers, not your hours (5 min)
2. "Are we working on the right things?" — priority check (10 min)
3. Introduce one forward-looking idea (5 min)
4. Confirm next 30 days + book the 90-day call (5 min)

**Script — opening:**

> "{ClientName}, two months in. Let me start with what I'm seeing on our side — {MetricResult, e.g. 'leads are up 18% against the baseline we set'} — and then I want to hear whether these are the right numbers for you to care about."

**Risk-surfacing questions:**
1. "Are these results showing up anywhere your leadership is noticing?" — *If no, your work is invisible where it matters. Fix the reporting line before renewal season.*
2. "If our priorities for the next 60 days could shift, what would you move up?" — *Detects scope drift and reprioritization. The client who answers "everything is fine" while their business pivots is a churn risk.*
3. "Who else internally should see these results?" — *Expands your stakeholder map. Single-point-of-contact accounts die when that contact leaves.*
4. "What's the one thing we could do better in the next 30 days?" — *Low-pressure framing that gets honest answers. Record it and fix it visibly.*

**What to listen for:**
- They redirect to cost/price talk → margin pressure is building. Don't discount on the call; note it and prepare a value-recap before the next invoice.
- They mention a team change on their side (new CMO, new hire) → re-onboard the newcomer immediately; new executives cancel inherited agencies.
- They ask "remind me what the original scope was" → scope memory is fading; resend the scope summary proactively.

**After the call:** Update the health tracker. A new stakeholder at the client = update the contact roster. A team change = flag "team change" as a watch item.

## THE 90-DAY CHECK-IN — "The Renewal-Seeding Call"

**Timing:** 90 days after kickoff. 30 minutes. On video.

**Goal:** Make renewal feel like the obvious next step. Review the full quarter, name the wins, agree the roadmap — and surface any remaining doubt while there's still time to act.

**Agenda:**
1. 90-day impact summary — before/after metrics (10 min)
2. "What did we get wrong, and what did we get right?" — honest review (8 min)
3. Next-quarter roadmap draft (7 min)
4. Confirm the renewal conversation timeline (5 min)

**Script — opening:**

> "{ClientName}, 90 days. Let me show you where we started versus where we are — {BeforeMetric} to {AfterMetric} — and then I want your honest read on what's working and what isn't, because the next 90 days should be better than these."

**Risk-surfacing questions:**
1. "If you were deciding today whether to renew, what would the deciding factor be?" — *Ask it plainly. The answer is your renewal playbook for the next quarter.*
2. "Is there anything you've been meaning to raise with us but haven't?" — *The direct invitation. Most clients will use it if you mean it.*
3. "How does our work compare to what you expected when you signed?" — *Expectation gaps are the #1 quiet killer. If the gap is "I thought it would be faster," you have a communication problem, not a quality problem.*
4. "Who else needs to be convinced for us to continue into next quarter?" — *Maps the renewal decision chain. Start building those relationships NOW.*

**What to listen for:**
- "Let's see how the next month goes" → soft no. Treat it as a save-play trigger: ask what's missing, offer a concrete 30-day sprint with defined outcomes.
- Silence on question 2 followed by body-language shift → there IS something. Say: "Take your time — I'd genuinely rather hear it now than after."
- They name a competitor → don't trash the competitor. Ask what specifically appeals to them, then show how you deliver it (or honestly say you don't).

**After the call:** Log everything. Update the health tracker. If renewal confidence is below 7/10, activate the Save Play (see File 03).

## THE QBR TEMPLATE — Quarterly Business Review

**When:** End of every quarter, for every client above your retainer threshold. 45 minutes. Video or in person.

**Goal:** Prove ROI, deepen the relationship, and open the upsell conversation naturally — from evidence, not from a pitch.

### Pre-Meeting Prep Checklist
- [ ] Pull quarter metrics vs. the goals set in the SOW
- [ ] Gather 2–3 specific wins with numbers (not adjectives)
- [ ] Identify 1 honest miss and what you changed because of it
- [ ] Prepare one forward-looking recommendation (the upsell seed)
- [ ] Confirm the right stakeholders are invited (decision-maker + day-to-day contact)

### Slide Outline

**Slide 1 — Title:** "{ClientName} × {YourAgency} — Q{Q} Business Review" + date.

**Slide 2 — Where We Started:** The baseline metrics from quarter start. One table, no fluff.

**Slide 3 — Where We Are Now:** The same metrics today. Show the delta in absolute numbers AND percentages.

**Slide 4 — The 3 Biggest Wins:** Each win = what happened, the number, why it mattered to their business. Use their language (revenue, pipeline, cost saved).

**Slide 5 — What We Learned (Including 1 Miss):** Name one thing that didn't work and what you changed. Honesty here is what makes slides 3–4 believable.

**Slide 6 — Next Quarter Roadmap:** 3–5 priorities, each with an owner and a target metric. Leave one row visibly "TBD — needs discussion."

**Slide 7 — The Opportunity (Upsell Slot):** ONE recommendation they haven't bought yet, framed as a gap in the roadmap: "To hit {Target}, the missing piece is {Service}. Here's what that would look like." One slide only — never a second pitch deck.

**Slide 8 — Open Discussion:** End with questions, not a summary slide.

### Metrics to Bring (pick the ones tied to their SOW)
- Revenue/pipeline attributed to your work
- Lead volume and lead quality trend
- Cost per acquisition / cost per lead vs. baseline
- Engagement/traffic/conversion metrics vs. baseline
- Project delivery: on-time rate, revision rounds
- Response-time SLA adherence (your side AND theirs)

### Rules
- Never present vanity metrics alone. Every number gets a "so what for your business" line.
- The miss on Slide 5 is mandatory. A QBR with no miss reads as a sales pitch and kills trust.
- The upsell slot is ONE slide. If they bite, schedule a separate scoping call. Never negotiate scope inside the QBR.
- Send the deck 24 hours before the meeting. No surprises.
